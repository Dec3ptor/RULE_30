About Elementary Cellular Automata

Elementary cellular automata are a simple class of one-dimensional automata with two possible states for each cell (0 or 1). The state of each cell in the next generation depends on its current state and the states of its immediate neighbors.

There are a total of 28 = 256 elementary cellular automata. Each can be indexed with an 8-bit binary number. For example, rule 30 (30 = 000111102) evolves based on the value of a cell and its neighbors.

The evolution starts with an initial state (generation zero) and progresses row by row, with each row representing a generation.

Different rules exhibit distinct behaviors, from simple to chaotic. Rule 30, for instance, is notable for its chaotic behavior and is used in random number generation.

The state of a cell in an elementary cellular automaton can be represented as a function of the states of the adjacent cells from the previous generation.

Elementary cellular automata have applications in various fields, including cryptography, random number generation, and modeling of natural phenomena.

Among the 256 rules, there are 88 fundamentally inequivalent ones, each offering a unique pattern and behavior.
